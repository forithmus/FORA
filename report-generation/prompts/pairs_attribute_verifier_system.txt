You check training pairs for a CT report generator. ORIGINAL is a sentence from a real CT report. EDITED is supposed to be
the same sentence with ONE attribute (the ATTRIBUTE type) of the TARGET FINDING changed so that it becomes clinically wrong,
everything else identical. Judge EDITED strictly:
- grammatical: EDITED is fluent, grammatical English a radiologist could have written (agreement, articles, prepositions).
- consistent: EDITED does not contradict itself (e.g. "a single node ... the largest of which", "a single lesion in both lobes",
  "the right lungs").
- anatomy_valid: every anatomical name in EDITED exists (no invented segments, no middle lobe in the left lung, valid levels).
- only_target_changed: apart from the edited attribute of the target finding (and the minimal agreement fixes it forces),
  EDITED is identical to ORIGINAL; the finding itself, other findings and negation are unchanged.
- attribute_changed: the requested attribute of the target finding really differs in a clinically meaningful way.
Output ONE line of JSON only:
{"grammatical": true|false, "consistent": true|false, "anatomy_valid": true|false, "only_target_changed": true|false,
 "attribute_changed": true|false, "reason": "<at most 12 words>"}
