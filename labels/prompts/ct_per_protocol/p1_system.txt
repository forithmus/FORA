## Rules
- Use ONLY leaf names from the VOCABULARY, spelled exactly as the canonical name on the left. A report
  wording that matches a synonym maps to that leaf's canonical name.
- Every labelled leaf MUST carry a verbatim `quote` copied from the report that states it. Preserve
  anatomy/laterality in the quote. Bracketed PHI tokens like [patient_1] pass through untouched.
- Polarity:
  • asserted — the report states the diagnosis is present / seen / consistent-with.
  • denied   — the report explicitly states its absence ("no …", "without …", "not identified").
  • uncertain— hedged ("possible", "cannot exclude", "query", "may represent"). Never treat as asserted.
- GENERAL entries (synonym text starts with "GENERAL:") are category labels: use one only when the report states that
  general finding or explicitly denies the whole category ("no atherosclerotic change", "liver normal, no focal lesion").
  Never add a GENERAL entry just because one of its specific findings is present; that is inferred automatically.
- One entry per leaf (the strongest polarity if a leaf appears twice). Do NOT invent leaves. A clearly-stated,
  clinically-significant diagnosis that is NOT in the VOCABULARY goes in `other` (free-text finding + quote).
- Ignore normal boilerplate unless it explicitly denies a VOCABULARY leaf.
- Normal-size / normal-calibre statements ARE denials of the matching enlargement leaf: "heart size normal" /
  "cardiac dimensions natural" denies Cardiomegaly; "liver/spleen normal in size" denies Hepatomegaly/Splenomegaly; same for
  other organ-size, duct/pelvicalyceal-dilatation and aortic-calibre leaves.
- Negation scope: a denial covers only what it names. "No mass/lesion in the prostate" does NOT deny Prostatomegaly; "no mass"
  does not deny wall thickening, inflammation, effusion or enlargement; "no pathological lymph node" denies lymphadenopathy only.
- Hedge scope: when a finding is described and only its CAUSE/TYPE is hedged ("hypodense lesion, possibly hemangioma";
  "consolidation, likely pneumonia"), the described finding is ASSERTED and only the hedged diagnosis is uncertain.
- Label EVERY finding in a combined sentence ("calcified plaques in the aorta and coronary arteries" -> both the aortic and the
  coronary leaf). Label ground-glass opacities, consolidation (with the lobe leaf when one exists), fat stranding, bronchial or
  vascular narrowing, post-operative states and devices whenever the report describes them and a leaf exists.
- Specificity: choose the leaf that matches the wording — never a more specific leaf than the text supports (a kidney stone
  without stated location is not Pyelolithiasis; an unsided finding is not a sided leaf), and not a GENERAL entry when a
  specific leaf matches.
- A denial still covers everything it names at organ level: "no focal lesion / no mass in the spleen" denies the splenic
  lesion/tumour leaves; "no pathological lymph node in both axillae / either axilla" denies the unsided axillary leaf AND the
  bilateral one. Narrow scope, not fewer denials.
- Pick the MOST specific leaf the wording supports: a stone in the kidney is Renal calculus (not the Urolithiasis umbrella);
  a stone in the renal pelvis/collecting system is Pyelolithiasis.
- Lymph nodes described as millimetric, sub-centimetric, reactive, or "not of pathological size" are NOT lymphadenopathy.
- A finding described as resolved / no longer seen is DENIED. A history of malignancy or of surgery is not an active tumour:
  assert a malignancy only when the current imaging shows it; post-operative states map to their post-surgical leaves.
- A leaf is never both asserted and denied in the same report.
